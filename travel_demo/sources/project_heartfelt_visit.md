# Heartfelt Visit

## Aim

Write a personal travelogue in the ordinary traveler's own register. Use plain, natural Chinese and keep useful colloquial particles, modest judgments, and rough edges from attributed user wording. When the chosen organization uses multiple moments, the piece should feel like a recollection of visiting the scenic area as a whole, with remembered scenes and human circumstances providing the substance. It does not need to become polished platform copy, literary prose, or a report.

The visitor's actual wording and emotional strength matter more than route coverage. Select the moments, visible details, contrasts, companion context, and small choices that make the memory personal. Omit weak or repetitive stops without explanation, but do not discard supported human detail merely to maximize concision.

## Material Organization

- Do not set a minimum or maximum word count. Let the amount and depth of traveler-confirmed material in the original interview transcript select the shape: one complete memory unit may be a title plus one concise sentence; one developed impression may use a small group of closely related details; several connected developments may carry one memory thread through multiple supported moments. Never create depth by elaborating one answer with synonyms or invented context.
- Stop when the chosen organization is complete. Never pad with generic scenery, invented actions, repeated judgments, or a ceremonial ending; do not cut supported personal detail merely to appear concise.
- Match the title's scope to the chosen memory. A detail-centered title fits a single moment; a piece using multiple scenes may use the place name, attributed traveler wording, or a confirmed overall impression. Avoid generic inventory titles such as “草堂两处”, “景区一游”, or “两段印象”. A plain place name is acceptable when no more personal title is honestly supported; never invent a poetic title or broad judgment to sound distinctive.
- Treat `Whole-Visit Context` as conditional material. When the traveler explicitly supplied an overall impression, include it once in the title or body wherever it fits most naturally, and let selected scenes support it when useful. If the title already expresses it, do not require the body to repeat it. When the traveler did not supply an overall impression, do not create one or force an overview of the trip.
- Let paragraph breaks follow a real shift in scene, time, perspective, or meaning. A single paragraph can be complete; multiple paragraphs are useful only when each develops the memory thread in a different way.
- A fuller transcript may support several scenes when they reveal different parts of the same experience. Do not mention another stop merely to increase coverage or move the visitor mechanically along the route.
- Do not open by explaining when the visitor left home or entered the scenic area. A confirmed whole-visit impression may briefly frame the selected scenes near the opening or ending, but do not add a generic summary. Do not close with the route home, subway station, wet shoes, or an invented assessment of the whole visit.
- Exact entry times, exit times, dwell durations, anchor order, and interaction order are backstage evidence. Omit them unless the user explicitly asks for a time-based account or one time fact is indispensable to the central feeling.
- If the title already states a confirmed whole-visit judgment, do not restate it in the opening sentence or search the materials for another broad judgment merely to fill the body. Remove sentences that repeat the title, the same memory, emotional label, or conclusion in different words. A brief transition is useful when it connects two supported scenes through a real contrast, companion, choice, or change in conditions.

## Voice and Form

- Treat attributed traveler wording as the primary voice source. Preserve meaningful qualifiers and particles such as “还算”, “有点”, “挺”, “就是”, “吧”, “没想到”, and “一般” when they fit. Do not replace them with more polished synonyms or stronger emotions.
- Preserving the traveler's voice does not mean pasting each interview answer as a separate sentence. Compress adjacent confirmed fragments into natural written Chinese: repeated subjects or nouns may be omitted, closely related clauses may be joined with ordinary syntax, and conversational fragments may be lightly reordered. Smoothing is syntactic, not narrative: do not add an unreported inner state, a new viewing or discovery step, a causal bridge, an accidental turn, or a stronger degree merely because it makes the paragraph flow. Do not introduce a new fact, motive, emphasis, or polished interpretation while smoothing.
- Let attributed humor land directly in the visitor's voice. Do not pre-explain it with reporting labels such as “当时开了句玩笑”, “我还调侃道”, or “我当时说”; keep or lightly punctuate a conversational particle only when it reads naturally in the finished note.
- Prefer first-person presence, direct judgments, and everyday sentence rhythms. Let the visitor say what seems worth remembering, but never manufacture a personal history or action to simulate sincerity.
- When confirmed, retain human circumstances such as who accompanied the visitor, who took a photo, waiting for a crowd to thin, deliberately stopping, or choosing what to remember. These details often distinguish a travelogue from a gallery caption; weave them into the scene instead of listing them as metadata.
- When an attributed overall impression is used, place it in the title or body and let selected moments make it concrete where that relationship is genuinely supported. When two confirmed moments are available without an overall frame, identify the richer personal memory as the center and let the other moment support it. Do not automatically assign one paragraph to each selected photo or introduce the piece as a list of places. Connect moments only through a user-supported perspective, expectation, reaction, or change in conditions; if no truthful connection exists, prefer the stronger moment and omit the weaker one.
- Keep feelings concrete and proportionate. Connect them to a supplied scene, photo, stop, interaction, user requirement, or reported experience instead of announcing abstract emotion.
- Use mostly plain words. For this style, literary freedom is limited to selection, compression, ordering, and ordinary connective syntax. Do not add metaphor, a quotable line, an inner monologue, a polished contrast, or an aphorism merely to make the piece feel authored.
- Organize around what remained in the visitor's mind, not around arrival, first stop, next stop, and departure. Selective chronology is allowed when a verified change—such as heat giving way to rain-cooled night air—creates the piece's meaningful contrast. Do not disguise a complete itinerary by replacing “首先／然后” with “本来／进门／后来／走到”.
- Do not add subheadings, checklists, emoji navigation, hashtags, ratings, or a ceremonial conclusion unless the user explicitly requests them.
- Public history or practical information may appear briefly when it changed the experience. Do not let background knowledge overpower the visitor's own encounter.
- A formal conclusion is not required. End on a supported scene, choice, or personal judgment when it gives the piece a natural landing; do not stop so abruptly that a developed interview reads like an incomplete caption.

## Truthful Feeling

- Do not invent a specific strong emotion or strengthen a supplied one. “还算比较惊喜” must not become “最难忘”, “深受触动”, or “记得最清楚”. When the user has not directly described a feeling, keep the response modest and grounded in defensible observations.
- Never invent weather, queues, conversations, companion behavior, previous visits, route trouble, fragrance, sound, departure details, clothing, bodily condition, or a personal action merely to make the emotion vivid.
- Avoid generic claims such as “治愈了内心”“不虚此行”“让人流连忘返” unless the user's own requirement clearly asks for that attitude and the wording is made specific.
- Do not end by extracting a general lesson about travel, life, cities, plans, guides, encounters, or beauty. Avoid balanced takeaway patterns such as “不在……而在……”.

## Failures to Avoid

- a primary-school excursion diary that recounts every stop with “先、然后、接着、最后”;
- the same itinerary diary hidden behind paragraph openings such as “本来、进门、真正让我、后来、走到”;
- a scenic-area introduction with a thin first-person opening and no personal response;
- stacked adjectives, classical allusions, grand life lessons, or deliberately beautiful sentences;
- social-media sales language, exaggerated “must visit” claims, checkbox attractions, hashtags, and engagement bait;
- treating every selected photo as a caption assignment;
- using a generic “两处／几段／一日游” title that merely counts the supplied scenes;
- turning two confirmed moments into two parallel photo captions without a narrative center;
- flattening a well-supported interview into generic summary sentences with no memory thread;
- deleting confirmed companion, waiting, choice, or contrast details that would make the account personal;
- claiming that dwell time or a photograph proves the visitor loved a place;
- vague emotional filler that could be attached to any scenic area.
- saying the same point again as a second paragraph or final sentence;
- replacing the traveler's casual wording with formal summary language;
- repeating the title's whole-visit judgment almost verbatim in the opening sentence;
- letting the title carry the only whole-visit judgment while the body contains only separate scene descriptions;
- stitching interview answers together with repeated subjects or nouns instead of shaping one natural sentence;
- inventing a casual-sounding inner state, review step, coincidence, or stronger comparison to connect confirmed fragments;
- explaining that a humorous line is a joke instead of letting the line appear naturally;
- reducing a whole scenic-area visit to one photographed pose when several scenes and an overall impression are confirmed;
- a tidy essay arc whose final line explains what the experience means.

## Contrast

Weak: “现场的意外让我明白，有些美好不在计划之中，而藏在不经意的相遇里。这也成为整趟旅程中最难忘的一刻。”

Better: “现场比照片里挤多了，等了一会儿才拍到。照片留着挺好看，至于景点本身，我觉得就还好。”
